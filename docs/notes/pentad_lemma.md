# The Pentad Lemma, and the $v = 120$ rung at $n = 7$

> **Superseded, by this repository's own later work.** The rung is now
> settled outright: `EGAN1P` **[EXH]** ([`pbound.md`](pbound) §13d) gives
> **`v = 120` ⟹ length ≥ 5908** = Egan(7) — one better than the 5907 that
> `RUNG0` **[THM]** (§5b) had already given by the residue-cap route, and
> thirteen better than the 5895 below. The same model settles 873 at n = 6
> and 46205 at n = 8. This note is kept because its argument is independent,
> elementary, and machine-checked by `code/rigidity7.py`, and because the
> Pentad Lemma it proves is used throughout. The headline number, though, is
> no longer the best we have.

**Result.** Every superpermutation on 7 symbols that enters exactly 120
two-loops has length $\ge 5895$. HPV gives only 5884 at that rung, and the
Hunter–Raudvere bound 5888 needs only 5888. So the tightest rung of the
$n=7$ ladder is clear by eleven units rather than the four required.

This is *one rung*. It is not a proof that $s(7) \ge 5895$, or even that
$s(7) \ge 5889$ — see [What this does and does not
prove](#what-this-does-and-does-not-prove) at the bottom. It is the exact
$n = 7$ analogue of the $n = 6$ rigidity argument that gave $s(6) \ge 868$,
and it is elementary: no Lean, no exhaustive cover enumeration (impossible
here — there are far too many covers), one new lemma.

Everything below is machine-verified by `code/rigidity7.py`, which rebuilds
the structure from scratch and asserts each claim.

## 1. Setup

Write permutations as bijections $[7] \to [7]$ and let $S_7$ act on the right.
Two standard moves:

$$\sigma(u) = u\cdot c \quad (c = \text{the 7-cycle on positions}), \qquad
\delta(u) = u\cdot d .$$

*Rotation classes* are the orbits $uC$, $C = \langle c\rangle$ — 720 of them,
seven permutations each. A *2-loop* is generated by "run a whole class, then
$\delta$": its six **generators** are $g, ga, ga^2, \dots, ga^5$ where

$$a = c^6 d, \qquad \mathrm{ord}(a) = 6 .$$

There are 840 two-loops; every permutation is a generator of exactly one of
them; each loop meets six classes, one generator apiece; seven loops pass
through each class.

An *arc* is a maximal weight-1 run of the Hamiltonian path; arcs live inside
rotation classes. With $R$ arcs the length bookkeeping is exact:

$$\mathrm{length} = 5045 + R + X, \qquad
X = \sum_{\text{jumps}} (\text{weight} - 2) .$$

## 2. The Stride Law, and there are exactly two cheap moves

From the end $\sigma^6 g_j$ of the arc of the $j$-th generator,

$$w(\sigma^6 g_j,\; g_{j+k}) = k+1 .$$

So inside a loop the only weight-2 move is $+1$ and the only weight-3 move is
$+2$. Off the loop, the arc end has exactly six weight-3 targets; one of them
is that $+2$, and **exactly one of the remaining five lands in a 2-loop
sharing no rotation class with the current one.** That one move is again
right multiplication by a fixed element:

$$\mathrm{om} = R_b, \qquad b = (3,4,5,6,2,1,7), \qquad \mathrm{ord}(b) = 6 .$$

(The reason everything is a right multiplication: relabelling the seven
symbols commutes with $\sigma$, $\delta$ and with overlap weight, and $S_7$
acts *simply transitively* on the 5040 permutations. Any equivariant map is
therefore $R_g$ for a fixed $g$. This also means one starting point is
representative of all 5040, which is what makes the checks cheap.)

## 3. The Pentad Lemma

> **Lemma.** In any Hamiltonian path in the $n=7$ permutation graph, at most
> **five** complete 2-loop traversals can be chained by weight-3 jumps.

A *complete traversal* is six full class-arcs of one loop joined by weight-2
moves: entered at generator $g$, it covers all six classes of $gA$ and exits
at $g a^5$.

*Proof.* Let a complete traversal of $L$ be followed at weight 3 by a
complete traversal of $L'$. $L$ has used all six of its classes, so $L'$ must
be class-disjoint from $L$; by §2 the only weight-3 target with that property
is $\mathrm{om}$. Hence the entry point advances by

$$g \;\longmapsto\; g\,a^5 b, \qquad \mathrm{ord}(a^5 b) = 5 .$$

After five steps the entry point is $g$ again, whose class is already
covered. $\square$

Five is attained: the five loops in one $a^5b$-orbit are pairwise
class-disjoint. So the lemma is sharp.

The lemma is *cover-independent* — it never mentions $v$, and holds for every
Hamiltonian path. That matters for the rungs above 120.

## 4. The $v = 120$ rung

At $v = 120$ the 120 entered loops carry $6 \times 120 = 720$ generator slots
for 720 classes, so the cover is **exact**. The Split Identity $R = 6v - A$
plus $R \ge 720$ (each class needs an arc) forces

$$A = 0, \qquad R = 720 :$$

every class is a single full seven-permutation arc, and
$\mathrm{length} = 5765 + X$.

Call a *visit* a maximal stretch of the walk inside one 2-loop, $V$ the
number of visits, and $f$ the number of visits that are complete traversals.
A loop visited once must be visited completely, so $f$ is exactly the number
of singly-visited loops and $t = 120 - f$ loops are visited at least twice,
giving $V \ge 120 + t$.

Every visit transition has weight $\ge 3$ (weight 2 would be the $+1$ move,
which stays in the visit), so it costs at least 1; a transition that is not
$\mathrm{om}$ costs at least 2. The $f$ complete traversals sit in at most
$(V - f) + 1$ maximal groups of consecutive ones, and the Pentad Lemma splits
each group into $\mathrm{om}$-stretches of at most five. Counting the
resulting expensive transitions:

$$X \;\ge\; (V-1) + \Bigl\lceil \tfrac{f}{5} \Bigr\rceil - (V - f) - 1
\;=\; f + \Bigl\lceil \tfrac{f}{5} \Bigr\rceil - 2 ,$$

and independently $X \ge V - 1 \ge 239 - f$. Taking the worst $f$:

$$X \;\ge\; \min_{0 \le f \le 120}\;
\max\Bigl( f + \bigl\lceil \tfrac f5 \bigr\rceil - 2,\; 239 - f \Bigr)
\;=\; 130 \quad (\text{at } f = 109, 110),$$

so $\mathrm{length} \ge 5765 + 130 = \boxed{5895}$ at $v = 120$.

The two branches are the whole content: keeping every loop in one piece
($f = 120$) costs $X \ge 142$ because the Pentad Lemma forces 23 expensive
jumps; breaking loops up dodges that but pays a visit each time. The optimum
for an adversary is to break about ten loops, and it still costs 130.

## 5. Why the naive floor is exactly HPV, and why it dies

Without the Pentad Lemma the same bookkeeping gives only

$$X \ge V - 1 \ge 119, \qquad \mathrm{length} \ge 5884,$$

which reproduces HPV at this rung on the nose — the weight-2 class edges form
120 disjoint directed 6-cycles, a Hamiltonian path uses at most 5 edges of
each, and $5\cdot 120\cdot 2 + 119\cdot 3 = 1557$ is precisely the HPV floor.
So the floor is not killed by any local obstruction: from every run end there
really are five weight-3 exits. It is killed globally, by $\mathrm{ord}(a^5b)
= 5$.

## What this does and does not prove

**Does.** Any 7-symbol superpermutation with $v = 120$ has length $\ge 5895$.

**Does not.** Anything about $s(7)$ on its own. A string of length $L$ has
$v \le L - 5764$, so ruling out length 5888 means clearing the rungs
$v = 120, \dots, 124$; ruling out 5905 means clearing $v = 120, \dots, 141$.
Rung 120 is done. Rungs 121–124 are open here.

For a complementary elementary proof of $s(7) \ge 5889$ what remains is slack
$\ge 4, 3, 2, 1$ at $v = 121, 122, 123, 124$; $v \ge 125$ is automatic from
HPV. At $v = 121$ the cover has surplus 6, so $A \le 6$, $R \le 726$, and at
most six classes are split into more than one arc. The Pentad Lemma still
applies verbatim. What is missing is a good lower bound on $f$: the current
one, $f \ge N_{\mathrm{full}} - 5S$ with $S$ the number of maximal full-arc
runs, only yields $X \ge 111$, against the 114 HPV already gives and the 118
needed. Either $f$ has to be pinned down better, or the Pentad Lemma has to
be extended to runs of four and five full arcs (the chaining maps $a^k b$
have orders $6, 4, 6, 5, 2, 5$ for $k = 0,\dots,5$, so short runs are
constrained too — just not by class-disjointness alone).

## Reproduce

```
python3 code/rigidity7.py
```

Rebuilds the 720 classes and 840 loops from the definition, verifies that
$\sigma, \delta, \nu, \mathrm{om}$ are right multiplications, verifies the
Stride Law and the two-cheap-moves count on all 5040 permutations, verifies
$\mathrm{ord}(a^5b) = 5$ and that its five loops are pairwise class-disjoint,
then evaluates the counting bound. `code/rigidity7_algebra.py` prints the
supporting group data (cycle types of all six $a^k b$, the six pairwise
class-disjoint targets of each loop).
