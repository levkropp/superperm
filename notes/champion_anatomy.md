# Anatomy of the optimum families

Generated by [`code/anatomy.py`](../code/anatomy.py) over the repaired corpus.
The target was: **what does 5906 do that no other optimum does?** The answer
turned out to be *nothing* — and that reframing is the result.

## 0. The corpus was badly incomplete

`census.py` read only the **first line** of every file. Files here hold
anything from one string to 42,288. Reading every line takes the corpus from
**177 to 44,564** measured strings:

| n | was | now |
|---|---|---|
| 5 | 1 | 188 |
| 6 | 7 | 44,126 |
| 7 | 169 | 245 |

Everything below rests on that repair, and one earlier conclusion does not
survive it.

## 2. The main finding — accidents are **not** the separator

The previous note conjectured that stitches/accidents are what carry a walk
past the Egan−1 line, on the evidence that all 43,096 standard-kernel n = 6
optima have `A = 0` while every n = 7 champion has `A ≥ 8`.

**The repaired corpus refutes it.** n = 6 has optima with `A = 2, 3, 4, 5` —
1,024 of them — and **every single one still sits exactly on the line**
(`v + Y = 29`). Accidents are available at n = 6 and buy nothing.

Claim `C6a` is retagged **[REF]** accordingly.

## 3. The real statement: it is a property of n, not of a string

| n | strings | below the line | optima | optima below |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | 188 | 0 | 9 | 0 |
| 6 | 44,126 | 0 | 44,121 | **0** |
| 7 | 245 | 239 | 237 | **237** |

At n = 6 **no** optimum is past the line — and since 872 is optimal, none can
be. At n = 7 **every** optimum is past it. So there is no special string to
dissect: 5906 is not an outlier among n = 7 champions, it is the whole family,
and the discontinuity is between n = 6 and n = 7.

That redirects the n = 8 question. It is not "find the one exceptional string"
but **"does n = 8 admit a k = 2 family at all?"** — a question about n, with
44,121 pieces of evidence that n = 6 does not and 237 that n = 7 does.

## 4. A new arithmetic pattern — **[MEAS]**

> At n = 7 every optimum has `A ∈ {8,9,10, 12,13,14, 16,17,18}`: groups of
> three spaced by `n−3 = 4`, with **residue 3 mod 4 never realised**.
> 237/237.

At n = 6 the spectrum is `A ∈ {0,2,3,4,5}` — only `A = 1` is missing, so there
is no matching residue rule there. Both are consistent with accidents arriving
in bundles rather than singly, which is the shape `a_cost_law.md` predicts
(stitches are *pairs* of adjacent pre-covered generators), but neither is
explained. Registered as `MOD4`.

## 7. Can we prove no 872 string has `A = 1`?

**Not yet — but the reason it is missing is now clear, and it is not a gap.**

Across the entire 44,564-string corpus, **every** walk with `A = 1` is an Egan
string:

| n | string | d | A | S | B | Y | length |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | `154-tight-egan` | 2 | 1 | 7 | 1 | 0 | 154 |
| 6 | `873-egan`, `873-tight` | 6 | 1 | 29 | 1 | 0 | 873 |
| 7 | `5908-egan` | 24 | 1 | 143 | 1 | 0 | 5908 |

All have `d = (n−3)!`, `B = 1`, `Y = 0`, `S = (n−1)(n−3)!−1` — the Egan vertex.
So the conjecture is not "A = 1 is impossible" but the sharper

> **`A = 1` ⟺ the Egan vertex** — registered as `A1EQ`, **[MEAS]**.

Since the Egan vertex has length 873 at n = 6, no 872 string can have `A = 1`,
which is exactly the single gap in the n = 6 optimum spectrum `{0,2,3,4,5}`.

**The existing machinery cannot prove it.** `a_split.min_T_at_A(6, v, 1)` per
rung:

| v | 24 | 25 | 26 | 27 | 28 | 29 | 30 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A=1 floor | — | 21 | 17 | **15** | 20 | 25 | 30 |

The minimum is 15 against the 29 needed, so the refined lemma is loose by 14
here and excludes nothing. Proving `A1EQ` would need a structural argument
about what a single accident forces, not the counting ladder.

## 8. Building champions from champions — what it does and does not buy

Both maps were checked directly on `houston_872` and `46204-raudvere`:

| map | superpermutation? | length | ledger vector |
|---|---|---|---|
| relabelling (n! of them) | yes | unchanged | **identical** |
| reversal | yes | unchanged | **identical** |

**Relabelling and reversal preserve the entire coordinate vector**
(`SYMM`, **[THM]** for relabelling: π acts on *values* while σ and δ act on
*positions*, so they commute and classes, loops, arcs and all edge weights are
carried across; reversal verified coordinate-by-coordinate at n = 6 and 8).

Two consequences:

1. **Yes, we can manufacture ~1,440 more 46204s** from the one we have (720
   relabellings × reversal, minus stabiliser). They are genuine, distinct,
   certifiable superpermutations.
2. **They add nothing to this analysis.** Every image has the *same*
   `(d, A, S, B, Y)`. The ledger coordinates are invariants of the symmetry
   orbit, not of the string. This is also what the Superpermutators forum
   thread's "4 solutions from 1" amounts to.

So the 8 distinct vectors at n = 6 and 9 at n = 7 represent **at least that
many genuinely different orbits**, and getting a second n = 8 vector requires a
different *construction*, not a symmetry. That is the honest cost estimate for
Track 6.

A caution about the rotation trick in the forum thread: sliding the string
around to restore the `12…n` prefix is only valid when the superpermutation is
cyclically closable. Applied blindly it corrupts the string — it made 872
measure as 873 here until the rotation was dropped.

   question. It is not about individual strings.
2. Explain `MOD4`, and the missing `A = 1` at n = 6. The stitch detector
   (`a_cost_law.md` motif) is the tool; it has not been built yet.
3. **Prove A2 — DONE.** `comps ≥ v − S` is a theorem: contract to the loop
   quotient and count its **cycle rank**, not its edges. The loop-contraction
   route recorded here as [REF] bounded the edges, which is the wrong question
   of the right object. [`pbound.md`](pbound.md) §8.
4. Does n = 8 admit `k = 2`? By A1 that needs `B+Y−A = 12`; the n = 7 family
   reaches 10 along `B = 10 + A`.
